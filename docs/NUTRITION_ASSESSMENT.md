# Nutrition-based calendar colours

The history calendar colours each day by **estimated nutrient intake compared with age-specific references**. It does not use the share of served food that was eaten; that remains a separate eating-habit metric in the reports.

The code lives in `src/nutritionAssessment.ts`, and the tests in `scripts/nutritionAssessment.test.ts`.

## Order of decisions for a day

1. **Red: meals not logged.** Any of the five scheduled meals is Not logged. Missing meals are never counted as zero intake, so no nutrition colour is computed, even when some estimates exist. The day sheet still lists the estimates so far.
2. **Grey striped: no food eaten.** All five meals were marked Not eaten.
3. **Grey: assessment unavailable.** The day is fully recorded, but either:
   - energy or protein can't be estimated, or
   - more than a third of the key nutrients lack enough data.
4. **Blue: above.** A key nutrient is above its upper limit, energy is above 120 % of the requirement, or protein supplies more energy than its AMDR. This is a caution, not "healthier".
5. **Yellow: below.** Energy is below 80 % of the requirement, or at least half of the assessed key nutrients are below their recommendation.
6. **Green: within.** None of the above applies.

Every percentage above can be changed. In the calendar legend, "Adjust assessment" sets:

- the low threshold
- the energy high threshold
- the share of foods that must have data
- the share of key nutrients that must be below for a "below" day
- which nutrients count as key

The settings are stored as the `nutrition-assessment` setting.

## Per-nutrient rules (not an average of percentages)

| Nutrient | Kind | Below | Within | Above | Over the limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy | requirement (EER) | < 80 % | 80–120 % | > 120 % | — |
| Protein | RDA, plus an AMDR ceiling | < 80 % of RDA | ≥ 80 % | > 150 % of RDA, or energy share above the AMDR | — |
| Carbohydrate | AMDR, % of energy | < 45 % | 45–65 % | > 65 % | — |
| Fat | AMDR, % of energy | < 30 % (1–3 y) / 25 % (4–6 y) | up to 40 % / 35 % | above | — |
| Fibre, omega-3 | RDA / AI | < 80 % | 80–150 % | > 150 % | — |
| Iron, calcium, zinc, vitamin C, vitamin D | RDA, plus UL | < 80 % | 80–150 % | > 150 % but ≤ UL | > UL |

**Key nutrients** decide the day's colour. By default they are energy, protein, fibre, iron, calcium, zinc and vitamin C.

- Vitamin D comes mainly from sunlight and omega-3 composition data is sparse, so both are shown but don't count by default.
- Carbohydrate and fat are shown as shares of energy but don't count by default.

### Upper limits

The upper limits are the US National Academies (IOM) Tolerable Upper Intake Levels:

| Age | Iron | Calcium | Vitamin D | Zinc | Vitamin C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 y | 40 mg | 2500 mg | 63 µg | 7 mg | 400 mg |
| 4–8 y (used for this app's 4–6 band) | 40 mg | 2500 mg | 75 µg | 12 mg | 650 mg |

ICMR-NIN 2020 publishes few upper limits for young children. These limits apply to food plus supplements, but the app counts food only.

### AMDR

The AMDR values are the IOM ranges:

| Age | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 y | 5–20 % | 45–65 % | 30–40 % |
| 4–18 y | 10–30 % | 45–65 % | 25–35 % |

### Sex

ICMR-NIN 2020 and IOM references for ages 1–6 are the same for girls and boys, so sex doesn't change the values. The child profile still records it for the growth charts.

## Missing data

- **Coverage:** a nutrient is assessed only when at least 90 % of the foods eaten (by count) have a value for it. Refused foods are excluded from the count.
- **Unestimable foods:** a food with no amount, no composition data or an unknown piece weight is listed under "Missing nutritional data".
- **Partial data as a lower bound:** when data is partial, the known amount is a lower bound. It can still settle the answer: a known amount already above the UL, or already at 80 % of the RDA, is reported with "≥". A known amount below the recommendation is reported as "insufficient data", never as "below".

## Food composition data

**Source:** `server/ifctData.ts` is generated by `scripts/ifct-extract.py` from the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 (NIN-ICMR), using the MIT-licensed npm package `@ifct2017/compositions`.

**How catalogue foods map to it:**

- 38 single foods use one IFCT food directly. That is raw composition; cooking losses of heat-sensitive vitamins are not modelled.
- 27 dishes are standard recipes built from IFCT ingredients and a cooked weight, e.g. rice 100 g raw → 300 g cooked.
- 22 packaged and processed foods (biscuits, chips, cheese, butter, oats, and others) are not in IFCT. They keep approximate values and are labelled "Estimate".

**Data corrections:**

- **Vitamin D:** only cholecalciferol (D3) is counted. IFCT lists ergocalciferol (D2) for some cereals at levels far above other databases, e.g. ragi 41 µg/100 g.
- **Energy:** where IFCT energy disagrees with the food's own macronutrients by more than 25 %, energy is recalculated from protein, fat, carbohydrate and fibre. This affects the chicken leg entry, among others.

**Units:** grams, ml, pieces, cups, bowls and spoons convert to grams through each food's own measures, or through standard household measures where a food has none.

## Disclaimer

The calendar, the day sheet and the reports state that these are estimates from recorded food, not a medical or dietary diagnosis. They note that one day varies a lot and that patterns over weeks matter more.
